The issue

Student health is the sustainability problem: loneliness, little time outside, and a week that treats exhaustion as normal. This page is for first- and second-year students who do not own gear or a car. Pell Grant recipients and students with Georgia Tech need-based aid can ask about a fee waiver.

Sustainability Next commits Tech to the full range of UN goals. Culture and the living learning campus are two of its six priorities. Cultivate Well-Being asks the Institute to expand health programs, and the sustainable living guide names ORGT and two hours a week outdoors.

What the numbers say

Georgia Tech’s Fall 2022 health survey had 318 respondents and a 4% response rate, so treat it as a warning light. Click a bar for the source. A 2019 study linked at least 120 minutes a week in nature with better reported health. That link is an association, not a prescription. One day trip can meet it.

What you get

Instruction, specialty gear, a van, and a two-to-three-hour class the week you leave. You bring food. You do not buy a boat, bike, or backpack. Many day trips are about $25–$80 and overnights about $80–$95. Register about a month ahead at mycrc.gatech.edu. Confirm the current price before you pay.
