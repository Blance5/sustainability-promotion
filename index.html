01 · The issue

Health is slipping between problem sets.

In Georgia Tech’s Fall 2022 health assessment, 56.6% of respondents screened positive for loneliness. About two-thirds of students in a later campus analysis said they had needed emotional support in the past year. A hard week is common here. It should not be the plan.

02 · Who we are

A program, not a club you have to crack.

We are a Campus Recreation program, not an invite-only club. You register for a trip. We bring the van, the instruction, and the specialty equipment. Trip leaders are Georgia Tech students who take safety seriously and still expect you to have fun.

03 · Your move

Two hours outside is a real target.

A University of Exeter study linked at least 120 minutes a week in nature with better reported health and well-being. Georgia Tech’s sustainable living guide points students toward that kind of time and names ORGT as a way to get it.

Written for

First- and second-year students who do not own a backpack.

This site is for undergraduates who are new to Atlanta, new to outdoor sports, or both. Trips are not reserved for people who already own a kayak or a car. Prices and prerequisites are in plain language, the first step is a beginner trip, and students with Pell Grants or Georgia Tech need-based aid can ask about a fee waiver.

If you are in crisis or you need clinical care, start with the Center for Mental Health Care and Resources. A trail is a health practice. It is not a treatment plan.
