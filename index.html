The issue

The sustainability problem here is student health under academic pressure: loneliness, uneven access to time outside, and a culture that treats a depleted week as normal. A campus can cut energy use and still be unsustainable if the people in it cannot stay well.

This page is for first- and second-year students who are new to Atlanta, new to outdoor sports, or both. You do not need a car, a kayak, or a friend who already camps. Prices and prerequisites are stated plainly, and Pell Grant recipients or students with Georgia Tech need-based aid can ask about a fee waiver.

President Ángel Cabrera described Sustainability Next as a plan to advance the full range of the UN goals across education, research, and operations. Culture and the living learning campus are two of its six priorities. The strategic plan’s Cultivate Well-Being focus asks Tech to expand physical, mental, and emotional health programs. The Office of Sustainability’s living guide then names ORGT for physical well-being and points to two hours a week outdoors for mental well-being.

What the numbers say

Georgia Tech’s Fall 2022 health assessment is a warning light, not a census: 318 students, a 4% response rate. More than half screened positive for loneliness. Only 37.7% met the federal guideline for both aerobic activity and strength training. Food insecurity touched 28.8%, which matters when a trip has a fee.

A 2024 campus report, using the Healthy Minds Study, found about two-thirds of students had needed emotional support in the past year and about one-third had received it. Diagnosed mental health disorders were 25% for cis men, 43% for cis women, and 70% for transgender and gender-nonconforming students. That last group was 23 people in the survey, so the rate is a signal to keep trips welcoming, not a precise campus count.

A 2019 Scientific Reports study of nearly 20,000 people in England linked at least 120 minutes a week in nature with better reported health and well-being. Less time did not show the same link, and the authors call it an association, not a prescription. One ORGT day trip is long enough to meet that threshold without owning a car or a tent.

The trips

ORGT is a Campus Recreation program, not a club you join by tryout. In a typical year we run on the order of 120 day and overnight trips, plus expeditions and training such as roll school and wilderness medicine. What registration includes

Instruction, specialty equipment, transportation, and a required two-to-three-hour class the week you leave. Most trips leave around 8 a.m. You bring food. You do not buy a backpack, boat, or bike for a typical first trip. Intermediate trips usually require a beginner trip in that sport first.

Recent public prices put many day trips roughly between $25 and $80 and many overnights around $80 to $95. Confirm the listing. Registration opens about a month ahead at mycrc.gatech.edu and fills first come, first served. Cancel early if you must: account credit is 100% at 25 or more days out, 50% from 24 to 14 days, and 25% from 13 to 7 days. If we cancel for weather, you get a full refund.

Then do this

Confirm you are a current student. Trips are funded by student fees. You must be 18, unless you are a student with a parent waiver.

Register on the official trip list. If money is the blocker, ask about the fee waiver at the Wilderness Outpost, 404-894-7420, during open hours.

Attend the pre-trip class. Paddlers also take the pool swim test.

After one trip, rent gear, join a weekly sport group, apply to CORE, or ask about leading. The program lasts because participants become instructors.

The Wilderness Outpost rents camping gear, flatwater boats, and bouldering kits. Pick-up is generally Thursday and Friday, 2–5 p.m.

Sport groups use the climbing wall, the pool, and weeknight meetings. You do not have to commit to an expedition to show up.

CORE is a season of weekends and a student-planned expedition. The published commitment fee has been $200, with aid available. Trip-leader training starts with a nomination after you have been on trips.
