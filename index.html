Outdoor Recreation Georgia Tech · Campus Recreation

A sustainable campus needs students who are well.

We are ORGT, the student-led outdoor program inside the Campus Recreation Center. For more than fifty years we have taken Georgia Tech students off the Hill for beginner-friendly adventure trips. This site is our case for why that work belongs in the conversation about sustainability: good health is not a reward for finishing the semester. It is part of how a campus lasts.

01 · The issue

Health is slipping between problem sets.

Georgia Tech students are capable and often active, and many are also lonely, stretched, and unsure where a real break fits. In the Institute’s Fall 2022 health assessment, 56.6% of respondents screened positive for loneliness. About two-thirds of students in a later campus analysis said they had needed emotional support in the past year.

02 · Who we are

A program, not a club you have to crack.

ORGT is not an invite-only outdoors club. We are a Campus Recreation program run by a large team of student, staff, and alumni volunteers. You register for a trip. We bring the van, the instruction, and the specialty equipment. Trip leaders are Georgia Tech students who take the work seriously and still expect you to have fun.

03 · Your move

Two hours outside is a real target.

Research led at the University of Exeter found that people who spent at least 120 minutes a week in nature were more likely to report good health and well-being. Georgia Tech’s own sustainable living guide points students to that kind of time — and names ORGT as a way to get it. We built a short matcher so your first trip fits your week.

Written for

First- and second-year students who do not own a backpack.

This site is aimed at undergraduates who are new to Atlanta, new to outdoor sports, or both. You might assume adventure trips are for people who already climb, already own a kayak, or already have a car. They are not. Our design choices follow that audience: prices and prerequisites are stated in plain language, the first step is a beginner trip, and students with Pell Grants or Georgia Tech need-based aid are pointed toward a fee waiver so cost is not the thing that keeps them inside.

If you are in crisis or you need clinical care, start with the Center for Mental Health Care and Resources. A trail is a health practice. It is not a treatment plan.
