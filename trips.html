The initiative

Instruction, a seat in the van, and a reason to leave campus.

ORGT is a community of students, staff, and alumni inside Campus Recreation, not a club you join by tryout. In a typical year we run on the order of 120 day and overnight trips, plus longer expeditions and training such as kayak roll school, climbing-wall sessions, and wilderness medicine. This page is about the part that serves the most students: open-enrollment trips for beginners.

What you are actually signing up for

A standard day or overnight includes expert instruction, specialty equipment, transportation, and a required pre-trip class of about two to three hours the week you leave. You meet the leaders, learn the basic skills, and ask the awkward questions while you are still on campus. Miss that session without contacting us and you cannot go, and you should not expect a refund. Most trips leave around 8 a.m. and are back before 10 p.m., though overnights obviously stay out.

You bring food, snacks, and money if the group stops to eat. You do not need to buy a backpack, a kayak, a bike, or a cave kit for a typical instructional trip. Group size stays small. Intermediate trips usually require a beginner trip in that sport first. Prices move by sport and semester — recent public listings have put many day trips roughly between $25 and $80 and many overnights around $80 to $95 — so read the listing on the day you register.

That package is why the program fits first- and second-year students. The van solves Atlanta logistics, the gear closet solves the startup cost, and a student leader lowers the intimidation. The fee waiver, for Pell Grant recipients and students with Georgia Tech need-based scholarships, is how we keep SDG 3 from becoming a perk.
